Record the review as records in report/: profile.json (the facts about the benchmark every section draws on), assessments.json (one row per check of the methodology, against report/assessments.schema.json), sampling.json (how the audited items were chosen), findings.json (filed by check id) and, once check_report has told you the derived verdict, prose.json (the short reader-facing blocks that verdict calls for, against report/prose.schema.json). check_report validates the records against the methodology and the template, derives the verdict into report/verdict.json and renders report/epoch_review.md; read it as its reader would before publishing. The rendered markdown is the deliverable, every block has an enforced word limit, and the verdict is computed from the assessments, never written by you.

report/methodology.md is the Epoch AI benchmark review methodology (report/methodology.yaml is the same as data): a reviewability gate (Full, Partial, or Inadequate, which stops the review at Not Enough Information), four minimum-standard checks each graded Pass, Flag or Not Reviewed (any Flag makes the benchmark Flawed), and seven evaluation-quality checks that inform but do not disqualify. Read it and the writing-epoch skill before planning: the check definitions, the 20% scoring threshold and the template's blocks govern what each row and section means. Keep figure data alongside the report.

The review runs in two phases, and each stage ends with a record written and check_report run, so the verdict is always derivable from what is on disk. Phase 1 is the task audit and reads no evaluation logs, whether or not the run holds any: until close_phase1 has frozen its labels, the logs tool refuses to read them and hawk_submit refuses a job that names them. Stage 0, orient, profile.json and sampling.json from the source, the documentation and the operator's overview, a sample drawn at random by someone else being random, recorded as its design with drawn_by naming the drawer; stage 1, reviewability, from the source alone; stage 2, items, the question-labels auditor and the substantive item checks on the sample, a two-item pilot first, with no logs attached to any item audit, then coverage.json and the scoring row written from it, the sampled defect rate against the threshold deciding it, and at most one repair batch for the items the batch left unassessed or unresolved, neither retried nor widened; stage 3, consistency, from the source's version history, its changelog and the published methodology; stage 4, close: with the labels in coverage.json and the reviewability, scoring and consistency rows written, a run that holds logs calls close_phase1 and goes on to stage 5 with no prose written, and a run without logs writes the prose blocks the derived verdict calls for and publishes, Flawed when scoring or consistency is flagged and otherwise Incomplete, with elicitation, bias and the log-dependent quality rows Not Reviewed and a notice the renderer prints under the header. The quality rows the source and documentation answer, contamination, human completability, construct validity and score range, are written in phase 1. Phase 2 is the effect analysis, in the same run once phase 1 closes or in a run that resumes a published phase-1 audit with logs attached, which closes it on restore; it never relabels the items phase 1 labelled. Stage 5, effect and grading, two jobs submitted together with the logs as their logs argument, neither retried nor widened: one gold-answer job over every item recorded as a defect, whose per-submission verdicts fill the Affected logs column and the footnotes with what each defect did to each recorded result, the submissions it leaves unjudged reported as unquantified; and one grading-labels audit over the whole sample, one agent per item, labelling each item's recorded grades, with at most one repair batch for the items it left open. Then export_coverage over the phase-1 batches and the grading batches together rewrites coverage.json, and the scoring row is rewritten from the union rate, an item defective as written or as graded counting once. When phase 1 closed on a scoring or consistency Flag, stage 5 runs the effect job alone and the review goes to stage 9. Stage 6, revisit: reviewability may rise on what the log headers disclose, and consistency is re-checked against the versions and graders the logs record. Stage 7, elicitation and bias in rubric order, from the headers and transcripts, each Flag with its impact; the first Flag ends the rubric, not the review: the rows after it and the quality rows are Not Reviewed, and the write-up follows. Stage 8, the log-dependent quality rows, when no row is flagged. Stage 9, the prose blocks the derived verdict calls for, then publish. Write as you go: the moment stage 2's labels exist, write the scoring row at its provisional level with the measure from coverage.json, and from then on every stage updates rows rather than saving them for the end; a row is never left Not Reviewed once the stage that answers it has run. Confirmation of candidate labels, extra sources and repairs of the last few items are refinements of a derivable review, not prerequisites for one: the working-time limit ends the run without warning, and a run that ends with records but no rows publishes nothing. When budget() shows half the working time gone, stop refining, write what the evidence supports, and publish; a published review that names its open questions beats an unpublished one. No attempts are ever generated: a review with no logs is a phase-1 page, and a benchmark job on the whole dataset is never part of a review unless the operator asks for it. Item audits are long agents, one per item: give an audit job max_connections equal to its item count, up to the cap, or the batch runs in waves. The auditors read item media; open an item's image yourself only to adjudicate a dispute about it, since a provider that refuses an image stalls the run. The scoring measure is computed from report/coverage.json over a sample selected for prevalence, recorded in sampling.json: when logs are supplied the population is the items they contain and the sample is a census of them or a random draw from them; a purposive sample establishes that a defect exists, not how common it is, and a scoring row that passes on purposive counts leaves the review Incomplete.
