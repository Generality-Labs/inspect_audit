Record the review as records in report/: profile.json (the facts about the benchmark every section draws on), assessments.json (one row per check of the methodology, against report/assessments.schema.json), sampling.json (how the audited items were chosen), findings.json (filed by check id) and, once check_report has told you the derived verdict, prose.json (the short reader-facing blocks that verdict calls for, against report/prose.schema.json). check_report validates the records against the methodology and the template, derives the verdict into report/verdict.json and renders report/epoch_review.md; read it as its reader would before publishing. The rendered markdown is the deliverable, every block has an enforced word limit, and the verdict is computed from the assessments, never written by you.

report/methodology.md is the Epoch AI benchmark review methodology (report/methodology.yaml is the same as data): a reviewability gate (Full, Partial, or Inadequate, which stops the review at Not Enough Information), four minimum-standard checks each graded Pass, Flag or Not Reviewed (any Flag makes the benchmark Flawed), and seven evaluation-quality checks that inform but do not disqualify. Read it and the writing-epoch skill before planning: the check definitions, the 20% scoring threshold and the template's blocks govern what each row and section means. Keep figure data alongside the report.

Work through the rubric in its own order, and let each stage end with a record written and check_report run, so the verdict is always derivable from what is on disk. Stage 0, orient: profile.json and sampling.json, from the source, the documentation and the operator's overview; a sample drawn at random by someone else is random, recorded as its design with drawn_by naming the drawer. Stage 1, reviewability: from the source and any supplied log headers alone. Stage 2, items: the question-labels auditor and the substantive item checks on the sample, a two-item pilot first; coverage.json follows and the scoring row is written from it: the sampled defect rate against the threshold decides it, with no logs read and no attempts run for it, and a Flag there ends the rubric and sends the review to stage 6. Stage 3, logs, only when scoring passes: read the supplied logs. If none were supplied, generate none: the review runs no attempts, consistency, elicitation and bias stay Not Reviewed, the renderer prints a notice saying so under the header, and the verdict is Flawed or Incomplete, never Verified; say in the methodology block that no logs were supplied. After the item batch returns, at most two more jobs, submitted together: one repair batch for the items the batch left unassessed or unresolved, and, only when the scoring row is a Flag and logs were supplied, one gold-answer job on the defective items alone with the logs as the logs argument. Neither is retried, followed up or widened to the whole sample. What the impact job returns is the scoring Flag's impact, the recorded submissions the defects misgraded, which the page prints as a footnote; the submissions it leaves unjudged are reported as unquantified, and a Pass carries no impact. When those jobs are collected, stage 6 follows. Stage 4, consistency, elicitation and bias in rubric order, from the logs' headers and transcripts, the task source and the published methodology, each Flag with its impact; the first Flag ends the rubric, not the review: the rows after it and all of stage 5 are Not Reviewed, and stage 6 follows. Stage 5, the quality rows, only when no row is flagged. Stage 6, the prose blocks the derived verdict calls for, then publish. Write as you go: the moment stage 2's labels exist, write the scoring row at its provisional level with the measure from coverage.json, and from then on every stage updates rows rather than saving them for the end; a row is never left Not Reviewed once the stage that answers it has run. Confirmation of candidate labels, extra sources and repairs of the last few items are refinements of a derivable review, not prerequisites for one: the working-time limit ends the run without warning, and a run that ends with records but no rows publishes nothing. When budget() shows half the working time gone, stop refining, write what the evidence supports, and publish; a published review that names its open questions beats an unpublished one. A benchmark job before the sample is fixed is premature; a benchmark job on the whole dataset is never part of a review unless the operator asks for it. Item audits are long agents, one per item: give an audit job max_connections equal to its item count, up to the cap, or the batch runs in waves. The auditors read item media; open an item's image yourself only to adjudicate a dispute about it, since a provider that refuses an image stalls the run. The scoring measure is computed from report/coverage.json over a sample selected for prevalence, recorded in sampling.json: when logs are supplied the population is the items they contain and the sample is a census of them or a random draw from them; a purposive sample establishes that a defect exists, not how common it is, and a scoring row that passes on purposive counts leaves the review Incomplete.
